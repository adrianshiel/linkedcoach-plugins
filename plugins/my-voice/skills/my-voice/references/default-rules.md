# Default rules

These rules cut the habits that make writing read as machine-written. They apply in every register, for the user and for anyone else Claude writes for, unless the user's Rules doc switches one off by number under "Default rules switched off".

They hold no voice. Voice comes from the user's Rules doc.

Each rule is marked **tell** or **style**. A tell is a habit that marks writing as machine-written whoever it's for, so it applies even when Claude writes in someone else's voice. A style rule is a sensible default for the user's own writing, and it doesn't apply in someone else's voice unless their profile agrees.

**D1. No em dashes.** (style) Use a comma, full stop, colon, parentheses or a new sentence instead.

**D2. Write full sentences.** (style) Don't clip the subject, verb or article out of a sentence.
- Bad: "Two reasons to do this." / "Happy to work around her schedule."
- Good: "There are two reasons to do this." / "I'm happy to work around her schedule."
- Join related clauses with "and", "but" or "so" rather than leaving them as separate punches. Exception: social posts, where the user's rules allow deliberate fragments.

**D3. Don't announce the next sentence.** (tell) Cut "Here's the thing", "Here's why that matters", "Let that sink in", "Let me explain". Say the point. If a signpost is really needed, ask the question and answer it.

**D4. No hedged announcements.** (tell) Cut "worth noting", "worth flagging", "it's worth mentioning", "one thing to call out", "importantly". If it's worth saying, say it.

**D5. No negated contrast added for rhythm.** (style) State the positive claim and stop.
- Bad: "They practise on accounts they're already working, not on made-up examples." / "It's not a tool, it's a system."
- Good: "They practise on accounts they're already working."
- This doesn't touch a real answer to an objection the reader is likely to have.

**D6. No AI vocabulary.** (tell) Avoid: delve, tapestry, leverage (as a verb), foster, robust, seamless, elevate, unlock, game-changer, navigate (figurative), landscape (figurative), realm, testament, pivotal, embark, journey (figurative), meticulous, underscore, multifaceted, paradigm, holistic, synergy, furthermore, moreover, "in today's fast-paced world". If the user genuinely uses one of these words, their Rules doc says so and that wins.

**D7. No filler "holds" or "carries".** (tell) "That holds", "if it holds" and "the profile carries weight" become "that's true", "if that's still the case" and "the profile has weight".

**D8. Name the thing.** (tell) Don't write "this is the part", "that's the part" or "the piece" for something that has a name. Write the headline, the offer, the first step.

**D9. Plain words over writerly ones.** (style) Write "got", not "pulled". Write "improve", not "sharpen". Write "situation", not "gap", and "type", not "shape". The plain version is often longer, and that's fine. The test is the reader's word, not the shortest word.

**D10. No imported idioms decorating an ordinary sentence.** (style) Write "whether the plan worked", not "whether it survived first contact with reality".

**D11. No warm-up openers in email.** (style) Cut "I hope this email finds you well", "I hope you're well" and "Just reaching out". The second line is the message.

**D12. No restart openers on a follow-up.** (style) Cut "just checking in", "just touching base", "circling back" and "just following up". Open on the actual question.

**D13. No flattering openers in replies.** (tell) Cut "Great question", "Absolutely!" and "You're right to ask".

**D14. No cute closers on a practical point.** (style) Write "If the answer is none, that makes it easier", not "that's the easiest answer of all".

**D15. No sentence that vouches for the writer.** (tell) State the fact and let the reader judge it.
- Bad: "I'm not asking you to do anything I haven't done myself. I built 28,000 followers here."
- Good: "I built 28,000 followers here using the same system."

**D16. Don't narrate the document.** (tell) Cut any sentence whose only job is to explain why a section exists or what's coming next ("I've included this so you can see...").

**D17. One plain hedge, never a stack.** (style) "I think", "probably" and "maybe" stay. "It could potentially possibly be argued" goes. Never remove honest uncertainty to sound more confident.

**D18. No hype and no invented numbers.** (tell) Cut "10x", "guaranteed", "effortless" and "transform". No statistic without a source you can name.

**D19. Lists only for real lists.** (style) Use a list for steps, options or separate items someone has to act on. Don't chop prose into bullets to look organised, and don't nest bullets in ordinary writing.

**D20. No reflex "One thing" opener.** (tell) Cut "One thing worth doing today:". "One more thing" is fine as a closing point.

**D21. No triplets for rhythm.** (style) Don't write three adjectives or three short phrases in a row just for cadence ("clear, simple and powerful").
